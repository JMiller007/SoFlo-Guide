VILLAGE OF KEY BISCAYNE · ISLAND SERVICE ATLAS

Life on the island
has its own map.

Village services meet county services at the water’s edge. The Village runs solid-waste pickup for single-family homes and duplexes; Miami-Dade provides water and sewer, and FPL handles electricity. Permits, flood questions, stormwater and public records each have a direct route below.

Single-family & duplex residents

One island, three collection rhythms.

Great Waste handles residential service for the Village. Condominiums and commercial properties arrange separate contracts through their property manager.

Garbage × 2Twice per week; one cart plus up to two 30-gallon bags at each collection.
Recycle · WednesdayOne blue cart weekly, across both sides of W. McIntyre Street. Keep recyclables loose, clean and dry.
Bulk · MonthlyCollection day follows your zone on the current 2026 map. Put items out by 6:30 a.m. and no more than 24 hours early.

Side-yard pickup is available when the cart and bags are accessible beside the home. Weekly yard debris has size, weight and bundle limits. Hazardous materials, construction debris and many appliance items need different arrangements.

COUNTY · WATER & SEWER

Use the county account desk.

Miami-Dade Water and Sewer manages household water and sewer service. The Village site directs residents to the County. For water conservation tools, leak alerts and usage, the Village also links to Miami-Dade’s WATER-SMART program.

VILLAGE · PROJECTS & PERMITS

Apply online, or plan a counter visit.

Building, Zoning and Planning accepts electronic permit and plan submittals through Accela and continues to accept paper submissions for scanning. Permit processing is Monday–Thursday, 7:30 a.m.–1:30 p.m.; inspections have their own daily window. Confirm requirements for floodplain, shoreline or tree work before construction.

Flooding, drainage or shoreline concern? Public Works covers stormwater, shoreline management and island infrastructure. The Village’s “Elevating Our Island Paradise” hub tracks resilience programs and active flood-mitigation projects.
